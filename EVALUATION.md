# Arabic model evaluation and choices

Research checked on 2026-10-11. These are my recommended open-weight candidates for this restaurant use case. They are **not** the hosted models used by the demo, and I have not run their weights or measured their latency. Published results, engineering estimates, proposed acceptance gates, and local application tests are distinguished below. A universal Arabic ranking would be misleading: Saudi calls, broadcast MSA and multilingual tool benchmarks measure different things.

## STT choice: Cohere Transcribe Arabic 07-2026

**Exact checkpoint:** `CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-arabic-07-2026`, July 2026 release. **License:** Apache 2.0. The official card identifies a 2B-parameter Conformer encoder/decoder supporting Arabic dialects, English, and Arabic-English code switching. Its July 7 leaderboard table reports average WER **25.87%**, SADA WER **37.47%**, SADA CER **23.53%**, and Common Voice WER **5.82%**. These are published results, not measurements from this project. [Official model card](https://huggingface.co/CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-arabic-07-2026).

This is my strongest STT candidate among the researched options because Saudi dialect coverage and bilingual speech directly match the task. The large difference between Common Voice and SADA results is a warning against advertising a single low WER as restaurant-call accuracy. The provider's release reports a 52.6% human preference over Whisper on code-switched inputs—a modest edge, not proof of flawless bilingual recognition. [Provider evaluation and methodology](https://huggingface.co/blog/CohereLabs/cohere-transcribe-arabic-07-2026-release).

**Alternative:** `openai/whisper-large-v3`, Apache 2.0 in its Hugging Face distribution; 1.55B parameters. Its mature multilingual ecosystem is attractive, but the card explicitly warns about uneven dialect performance, hallucinated text and lack of native real-time transcription. I would retain it as a reproducible baseline rather than assume older multilingual averages establish Saudi accuracy. [Whisper large-v3 card](https://huggingface.co/openai/whisper-large-v3).

**Hardware estimate:** start with one 16–24 GB NVIDIA GPU, FP16/BF16, batch size one and short utterances; the 2B weights alone are about 4 GB at 16 bits, with substantial additional activation/runtime memory. This is a planning estimate, not a measured minimum. Start with a latency hypothesis of 0.5–2 seconds after a 3–8 second utterance on a warm GPU, then benchmark p50/p95. Target real-time factor ≤0.25 and p95 endpoint-to-transcript ≤2 seconds for that utterance range. Do not describe offline chunking as native streaming; genuine streaming/VAD needs its own evaluation.

**Test plan:** use a fixed speaker-disjoint SADA test subset, Common Voice Arabic as an MSA check, and Arabic-English portions of Casablanca. Casablanca exists specifically to expose dialect/code-switching gaps; its paper shows that domain and dialect specialization can change rankings. [Casablanca dataset paper](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.1211/). Add 300 consented restaurant utterances from at least 15 Saudi speakers, split across Najdi/Hijazi, quiet/noisy conditions, digits, food names, modifiers and corrections. Keep 100 utterances held out until the final comparison. Simulate 8 kHz telephone bandwidth and packet/noise conditions separately from clean microphone recordings.

Report raw and normalized WER/CER; normalize diacritics, Alef forms and digits consistently, but separately score quantities, negations, item names and addresses so normalization cannot hide a wrong order. Proposed in-domain gates: normalized WER ≤15% clean / ≤25% noisy, quantity and negation preservation ≥98%, menu/zone entity exact match ≥95%, and zero silent hallucinations that progress into an order. These are desired thresholds; failure leads to clarifications or a different model, not rewritten results.

## LLM choice: Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507

**Exact checkpoint:** `Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507`, July 2025 non-thinking instruction release. **License:** Apache 2.0. The card lists 30.5B total / 3.3B activated parameters, native 262,144-token context, BFCL-v3 **65.1**, MultiIF **67.9**, and MMLU-ProX **72.0**. These aggregate multilingual/tool scores support investigating it as an agent model; they are not Arabic restaurant success rates. [Official card](https://huggingface.co/Qwen/Qwen3-30B-A3B-Instruct-2507).

My selection weights multilingual understanding, tool use and interactive latency together. Qwen3's report describes broad multilingual coverage; that supports an Arabic candidate shortlist, but does not establish Saudi dialect reliability for this exact checkpoint. [Qwen3 technical report](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.09388). I would choose this model for the first controlled open-weight deployment trial, contingent on the task gates below, rather than claim it wins every Arabic benchmark or is the newest release.

**Alternative:** `humain-ai/ALLaM-7B-Instruct-preview`, revision `v2` / `7b-alpha-v2.33.0.30`, Apache 2.0 as listed on its official card. It is explicitly Arabic/English focused and smaller, with a 4,096-token context. Its Arabic specialization is attractive for local expression, but the short context and lack of comparable tool-call evidence in the inspected material make it a riskier default for a menu/tool conversation. [ALLaM card and revision tags](https://huggingface.co/humain-ai/ALLaM-7B-Instruct-preview). This is an engineering tradeoff, not a measured head-to-head result.

**Hardware estimate:** the full 30.5B weights must be stored even though only 3.3B activate per token. Plan an 80 GB GPU for BF16 with modest concurrency, or validate a 4-bit deployment on 24–48 GB with a deliberately short 8K context. Quantization changes quality and needs another run. Planning latency hypothesis: 0.3–1.5 seconds TTFT and 30–80 output tokens/second on a warmed suitable GPU, batch one, short context. Neither number was measured here. Proposed gate: p95 TTFT ≤1.5 seconds, ≥30 output tokens/second and p95 complete tool turn ≤5 seconds at the declared load.

**Test plan:** use ArabicMMLU for a broad Arabic comprehension sanity check, AraDiCE Gulf subsets for dialect/cultural probes, then prioritize a purpose-built restaurant suite. Neither broad benchmark replaces tool/journey tests. [ArabicMMLU](https://huggingface.co/datasets/MBZUAI/ArabicMMLU), [AraDiCE](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.11404).

Create 100 scenario scripts with five paraphrases each, including «أبي/أبغى», spelling variations, ٢/2, «large meal بدون صوص», one-word answers, corrections, ambiguous references, off-menu requests, unsupported zones, branch changes, complaints, invalid tools and adversarial requests to ignore prices. Run baseline and candidate five times at fixed declared decoding settings. Score final order exact match, task completion, argument/schema validity, entity extraction, clarification rate, context tokens, TTFT and throughput. Proposed gates: ≥95% task completion, ≥99% valid tool arguments, and **zero** unconfirmed/duplicate orders, wrong totals or unavailable-item checkout. Safety assertions come from code/state, not an LLM judge. Native reviewers should rate naturalness ≥4/5 and confirm that terse answers retain meaning.

## TTS choice: Chatterbox Multilingual V3

**Exact model/version:** `ResembleAI/chatterbox`, **Chatterbox Multilingual V3**, loaded with `ChatterboxMultilingualTTS.from_pretrained(..., t3_model="v3")` and `language_id="ar"`. **License:** MIT. The official card lists a 0.5B multilingual model, Arabic among 23 supported languages, and a V3 update intended to reduce repetition/hallucination and improve speaker similarity. [Official card and V3 loading instructions](https://huggingface.co/ResembleAI/chatterbox).

This is my preferred open TTS candidate for the Arabic trial because it combines explicit Arabic support with controllable speech generation and permissive licensing. The evidence is weaker than for the STT choice: the inspected card does not establish Saudi MOS, menu-name accuracy, or an Arabic-only advantage over all alternatives. General/English preference claims are not Arabic quality scores. I would not sign off for customers without a blind Saudi listening test. Keep the model's generated-audio watermarking.

**Alternative:** `coqui/XTTS-v2`, v2, licensed under the **Coqui Public Model License**, not MIT/Apache. Its card includes Arabic among 17 supported languages. It provides an established multilingual comparison, but licensing conditions need review for the intended commercial use and its Arabic restaurant pronunciation still needs testing. [XTTS-v2 card](https://huggingface.co/coqui/XTTS-v2).

**Hardware/latency estimate:** start with an 8–12 GB GPU, batch one, short clauses, and measure actual peak allocation. Planning hypothesis: 0.5–2 seconds time to first audio and RTF 0.3–0.8 with warmed weights on suitable hardware. These are unmeasured estimates. Proposed gates: p95 first audio ≤1.5 seconds, RTF <1, and no truncated checkout details. If a chosen runtime only emits complete files, report full generation delay instead of claiming streaming first-audio latency.

**Test plan:** build 200 fixed utterances from the provided 144 menu names, required option names, quantities, prices with decimals, Riyadh/Jeddah addresses, order IDs, Saudi conversational phrases and mixed-language sizes. Recruit at least five native Saudi listeners, blind and randomized across model/voice/configuration. Use only consented reference voices if voice conditioning is used. Each sample gets ≥3 ratings. Measure MOS, pairwise preference, critical-token accuracy, omitted/repeated words, exact listener transcription of order IDs, latency and RTF. ASR round trips can flag errors but are not a substitute for ears. Proposed gates: MOS ≥4/5, ≥98% item/quantity/price accuracy, 100% correctly recoverable test order IDs, and no invented words in checkout summaries. Investigate every critical mismatch.

## Reproducibility and measured status

Before running open weights, record the resolved Hugging Face commit SHA, exact runtime/package versions, license file, quantization, GPU/driver, batch size, warmups, decoding parameters and dataset split hashes. Model version names above identify the researched releases; floating repository heads must not become production pins. No open-model performance measurements are claimed by this repository.

The local application suite tests deterministic state/tools and mocked failure behavior. `scripts/live_eval.py` separately runs hosted-model conversations and optionally a **synthetic** Arabic audio round trip. Reports retain expected inputs, observed transcripts, tool calls, final state, failures and timings. Real-speaker STT accuracy, native-listener TTS scores, streaming latency and broad model comparisons remain separate research tasks. See [TESTING.md](TESTING.md) for the current execution status and the mandatory human demo checks.
